# Question 1

## How do you prioritize your work?

______________________________________________________________________

### Weak Answer

> I complete tasks one by one.

______________________________________________________________________

### Better Answer

> I first understand business priorities, deadlines, and dependencies. Then I organize my work based on customer impact and communicate any risks early if priorities change.

______________________________________________________________________

### Excellent Answer

> I usually begin by understanding the business objectives behind each task. Production incidents and customer-facing issues naturally receive immediate attention. After that, I consider delivery deadlines, dependencies with other teams, and technical complexity. I also regularly communicate with stakeholders whenever priorities shift to ensure everyone has the same expectations.

# Question 2

## What if you have multiple urgent tasks?

______________________________________________________________________

### Weak Answer

> I work harder.

______________________________________________________________________

### Excellent Answer

Situation

> During one sprint we had a production issue, an approaching feature deadline, and several code reviews waiting.

Action

> I first evaluated customer impact. The production issue affected active users, so it became the immediate priority. I informed my manager about potential delays to feature work, delegated code reviews where appropriate, and resumed feature development once production was stable.

Result

> Customer impact was minimized while stakeholders remained informed about adjusted timelines.

Learning

> Prioritization requires communication as much as technical decision-making.

# Question 4

## Have you ever missed a deadline?

______________________________________________________________________

### Weak Answer

> No.

Few interviewers

believe this.

______________________________________________________________________

### Excellent Answer

Situation

> Early in my career I underestimated the complexity of an integration task.

Action

> Once I recognized the risk, I informed the team immediately, updated the estimate, and proposed delivering the highest-priority functionality first.

Result

> The most important business requirements were delivered on time, while the remaining work was planned for the following iteration.

Learning

> Early communication is much more valuable than trying to hide schedule risks.

______________________________________________________________________

# Question 5

## What do you do when priorities change?

______________________________________________________________________

### Excellent Answer

Situation

> Midway through development, a production issue required immediate attention.

Action

> I paused lower-priority work, discussed revised priorities with my manager, documented my progress, and resumed feature development once the incident was resolved.

Learning

> Flexible planning allows engineers to respond quickly without losing long-term progress.

# Question 7

## How do you manage interruptions?

______________________________________________________________________

### Excellent Answer

> I first determine whether the interruption is genuinely urgent. Production incidents or customer-impacting issues take priority. For less urgent requests, I communicate my current commitments and schedule time to address them without disrupting existing deadlines.

______________________________________________________________________

# Question 8

## How do you balance quality and speed?

______________________________________________________________________

### Excellent Answer

> My goal is to deliver the highest business value while maintaining an appropriate level of engineering quality. Not every feature requires the same level of optimization, but reliability, testing, and maintainability should never be ignored.

# Managing Technical Debt

Question

"When do you fix technical debt?"

Good answer

> I evaluate technical debt based on business impact and future maintenance cost. Critical debt affecting reliability or delivery should be addressed early, while lower-impact improvements can be planned alongside feature work.

# Interview Deep Dive

## Question

What do interviewers expect from prioritization questions?

### Answer

They expect engineers who can balance customer impact, business priorities, technical quality, and communication. Strong
candidates explain how they make decisions rather than simply saying they "work hard."

______________________________________________________________________

## Question

Is it okay to admit that priorities change?

### Answer

Yes. In software engineering, changing priorities are normal. Interviewers want to know how you adapt while keeping
stakeholders informed and maintaining delivery quality.

______________________________________________________________________

## Question

How should Senior Engineers think about time management?

### Answer

Senior engineers focus on delivering business value, communicating proactively, managing dependencies, and continuously
reassessing priorities rather than simply completing tasks in the order they arrive.
