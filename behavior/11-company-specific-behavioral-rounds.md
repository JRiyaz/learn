# Company-Specific Behavioral Interviews

One of the biggest mistakes candidates make is giving the **same behavioral answers** to every company.

While the questions may sound similar, different companies evaluate different qualities.

For example:

- Amazon values ownership and customer obsession.
- Google values collaboration and problem-solving.
- Microsoft values growth mindset and teamwork.
- Meta values speed and impact.
- Startups value ownership and adaptability.

The goal is **not** to invent different stories.

Instead, you'll learn how to emphasize different aspects of the **same story** depending on the interviewer.

______________________________________________________________________

# The Same Story, Different Focus

Consider your **Microservices Migration** project.

Depending on the company, you'd tell the same story differently.

| Company | Emphasis |
|----------|-----------|
| Amazon | Ownership and Bias for Action |
| Google | Technical reasoning and collaboration |
| Microsoft | Teamwork and learning |
| Meta | Execution speed and impact |
| Startup | Initiative and wearing multiple hats |

This is exactly how experienced candidates interview.

# Final Advice

Before every interview, ask yourself:

What does this company value most?

Then adjust your emphasis accordingly.

You don't need different experiences.

You simply highlight different parts of the same experience.

For each company, answer:

1. Tell me about ownership.
1. Tell me about leadership.
1. Tell me about a failure.
1. Tell me about a difficult project.
1. Tell me about innovation.
1. Tell me about teamwork.
1. Tell me about learning.
1. Tell me about technical decisions.
1. Tell me about improving reliability.
1. Tell me about solving a customer problem.

Try answering each question twice:

- Once as if interviewing at Amazon.
- Once as if interviewing at Google.

Notice how the emphasis changes while the story remains the same.

# Interview Deep Dive

## Question

Tell me about a time you demonstrated ownership.

### Answer

> A good example is when I noticed that authentication logic had evolved differently across multiple backend services. While each implementation worked individually, the inconsistencies increased maintenance effort and contributed to production issues over time. Rather than continuing to fix individual bugs, I stepped back to understand the broader pattern and proposed building a reusable Authentication & Authorization Framework.

> I analyzed the common authentication flows, identified opportunities for standardization, and designed a framework that emphasized consistency, security, and ease of adoption. I worked with the team to integrate it incrementally so existing services could migrate without significant disruption.

> After adoption, authentication-related production incidents dropped by around 40%, and future services were able to reuse the framework instead of implementing authentication independently. The biggest lesson for me was that ownership often means solving the underlying problem rather than repeatedly addressing its symptoms.

# Summary

Behavioral interviews are not one-size-fits-all.

The strongest candidates understand **what each company values** and adapt the emphasis of their stories accordingly.

Remember:

- **Amazon** → Ownership, Customer Obsession, Dive Deep
- **Google** → Collaboration, Technical Judgment, Learning
- **Microsoft** → Growth Mindset, Teamwork, Empathy
- **Meta** → Execution, Speed, Impact
- **Startups** → Ownership, Adaptability, Initiative

You don't need different stories for each company—just different perspectives on the same experiences.
